# Fly Trainer teaching programme

Implemented learning path, 8 November 2026. The programme assumes no genetics knowledge on entry and progresses to integrated research reasoning. Every module remains directly accessible; recommended preparation guides rather than locks the learner.

## How a taught module works

1. **Explain.** Introduce the biological question, chromosome objects, vocabulary and assumptions. Use a visual explanation before asking the learner to calculate or select.
2. **Demonstrate.** Follow a complete example through four learner-controlled stages. Known reference examples can show their genotypes. Calculated examples use the same pure models as the later activities; mechanism-only examples are labelled as schematic.
3. **Guide.** Give a reasoning checklist with the first activity of a new type. Feedback explains the genetic reason for an error.
4. **Practise.** Remove the checklist and change the problem, constraint or evidence. Optional worked support remains available; completion using explicit solution support is distinguished from independent practice where recorded.
5. **Diagnose.** Interpret an unexpected result and choose a discriminating follow-up. Connect the conclusion to its assumptions and alternative explanations.

Experienced learners can start at an activity. Skipped explanation pages are marked separately from viewed pages. Worked stages, activity inputs, guide preferences and construction histories save locally. Watching an example does not answer the next question or build the target stock.

## Stage 1: first contact

| Module | Teaching and activity | Evidence to discuss with the learner |
|---|---|---|
| Meet the fly | Guided 3D inspection, manipulation and sexing | Uses foreleg anatomy and can explain uncertainty in appearance. |
| From a vial to a reliable cross | Egg, larva, pupa and adult; generations; source and stock records | Distinguishes a parent from F1 and sex identification from virginity evidence. |
| Virgin collection | Sort a batch from a trusted clearing; delayed review | Selects eligible females and justifies collection from records rather than colour alone. |
| Start here: the language of genetics | Chromosome, locus, allele, genotype, phenotype, dominance and gamete | Can describe the objects without confusing a chromosome with a gene or a pair with two flies. |

## Stage 2: read and predict

| Module | Teaching and activity | Evidence to discuss |
|---|---|---|
| Chromosomes and inheritance | Segregation, fertilisation, independent assortment, viability | Builds gametes and derives adult shares from viable combinations. |
| The X chromosome | Maternal and paternal X transmission, hemizygosity, reciprocal crosses | Traces each chromosome and explains a forbidden offspring class. |
| First cross | Collect parents, predict, remove parents, score F1 and investigate | Connects source history, paternity and observed offspring without inventing an individual hidden mating history. |
| Read a fly stock and a cross | Slashes, semicolons, +, Y, marker/chromosome names and cross direction | Translates records to diagrams and states what a visible marker does not establish. |

## Stage 3: build and verify

| Module | Teaching and activity | Evidence to discuss |
|---|---|---|
| Balancers | Marker, lethality and inversions; female versus male meiosis; marked intermediates | Preserves an unmarked chromosome rather than selecting an indistinguishable mixture. |
| Cross planner | Six progressive construction puzzles with optional hints; begin with 1–2 and return to 3–6 after their relevant driver/recombination teaching | Plans generations and declares a class only when its composition supports the target. |
| Counting and chance | Expected counts, repeated samples and chi-square | Separates fit from proof, considers sampling and distinguishes ratio shifts from impossible classes. |
| Recombination and mapping | Phase, reciprocal products, recovery, balancing and verification | Counts both recombinant products and separates marker dosage from functional validation. |

## Stage 4: design experiments

| Module | Teaching and activity | Evidence to discuss |
|---|---|---|
| GAL4/UAS and controls | Inheritance versus expression; driver/responder/sibling and independent controls | Chooses controls for competing explanations rather than treating a sibling as genetically identical. |
| Complementation and deficiency mapping | Functional copies, controls, inclusion/exclusion and candidate follow-up | Reads the informative class and distinguishes regional mapping from causal identification. |
| Three-point mapping | Reference phase, parental/DCO classes, interval totals, coincidence and interference | Solves a fresh phase/count sample and states viability and scoring assumptions. |
| FLP/FRT clones and twin spots | Reciprocal products, induction time, lineage growth and independent tissues | Uses the spread across discs rather than one attractive patch. |
| GAL80ts timing | Retained DNA, repression, activation, protein depletion and recovery | Checks the complete biological assay window, with temperature and knockdown validation. |
| Planning a screen | Independent chromosomes, coverage, conditional mutation burden and workload | Solves constraints together and explains what the probability estimate depends on. |
| Choose the genetic strategy | Forward/reverse genetics, loss/gain effects, reporter versus protein readouts, MARCM and maternal contribution | Matches the readout to the question and selects evidence distinguishing the main alternative. |

MARCM, maternal/germline contributions and reporter/tag distinctions are taught as mechanisms. The stock builder does not pretend to simulate transgenesis, all germline-clone viability requirements or every possible segregation outcome.

## Stage 5: integrated investigations

**Do not lose the invisible chromosome.** Diagnose a plausible failed route, then build the unmarked lethal with a driver and two balancers. The learner chooses actual crosses and kept classes over two generations; mistakes can leave ambiguous descendant classes. Correctness requires constructing the target, not choosing a description of the route.

**Construct, verify and interpret.** Build a reporter-bearing stock with a driver–RNAi recombinant over a balancer. The three-generation mission combines female recombination, parallel intermediates, marker dosage and chromosome tracking. Follow construction with validation/control/timing decisions and an optional written research argument. Notes are retained for the learner or a supervisor; their scientific quality is not automatically graded.

Mission histories save decisions rather than an exposed hidden-genotype key. Restoring a claimed success replays the construction and checks the solved target again.

## Reference alignment

Material was cross-checked against the local author-provided references, with paraphrased explanations and original application diagrams:

- **A. Prokop, A rough guide to Drosophila mating schemes, student/lite v5.2** (`FlyGenetics_IntroStudents_v5.2-format.pdf`): sections 3–4 for handling and inheritance, section 5 for transgenes, section 6 for mapping, and Box 5 for designing schemes.
- **A. Prokop, full guide v6.2** (`Roote Prokop-SupplMat-1v6.2-format.pdf`): Box 4 on preserving stock information; sections 4–6 and Box 9 on cross design; section 5.2 and Figures 13–16 on reporter/protein tools, GAL4/UAS, MARCM and maternal contributions.
- Existing model caveats remain alongside the lessons. The guides provide context, not universal timing or maintenance protocols for every strain and laboratory.

## Validation and interpretation of progress

Automated checks establish that the software renders and that model-derived answers, replayed constructions and saved states behave as intended. They do not measure learning transfer. A completed module records activity; fresh-case performance and construction evidence are more specific observations, not certification of laboratory expertise.

For a human pilot, ask a complete beginner, an intermediate learner and an experienced fly researcher to explain their reasoning on unfamiliar cases. Review identification on photographs/real specimens, a novel stock-building plan and a diagnostic experimental argument separately. Use the recorded notes to identify vocabulary gaps and unjustified inferences. Real laboratory competence requires supervised practical experience and independent validation.
